Design a premium, modern, mobile-first e-commerce web application for a personal fashion brand called **“The Haya Collection.”**

The website will be a **Progressive Web App (PWA)**, so the mobile experience is extremely important. On mobile, the website should feel less like a traditional responsive website and more like a beautifully designed, polished native mobile shopping application. Desktop should feel like the same product expanded onto a larger canvas rather than a completely different website.

The goal is to create an e-commerce experience that feels like the **future of online shopping**: elegant, intuitive, fast, visually refined, highly interactive, and thoughtfully designed down to small details.

Do not make it look like a generic Shopify/WooCommerce/marketplace template. It should retain familiar e-commerce conventions so users immediately understand how to use it, while improving the experience through thoughtful UX, modern layouts, micro-interactions, transitions, animations, progressive disclosure, contextual information, and excellent visual hierarchy.

The brand should feel **clean, royal, sophisticated, modest, premium, and modern** without becoming overly luxurious, flashy, or complicated.

Brand:

- Name: The Haya Collection
- Category: Personal fashion / modest fashion e-commerce
- Business scale: Small independent brand initially, but the design should be capable of scaling into a much larger premium fashion store.
- Primary acquisition channel: Social media, especially Instagram.
- Customers will often arrive directly on a product page from an Instagram post/reel/story, so product pages must work extremely well as landing pages.
- The experience should build trust quickly because many customers may be discovering the brand for the first time.

DESIGN PHILOSOPHY

Create a visual identity that combines:

- Royal elegance
- Modern minimalism
- Modest fashion aesthetics
- Premium editorial fashion design
- Clean typography
- Generous whitespace
- Strong product photography
- Subtle depth
- Refined interactions
- Sophisticated but restrained animations

Avoid:

- Generic marketplace layouts
- Excessive gradients
- Excessive glassmorphism
- Overly bright colors
- Clutter
- Too many cards everywhere
- Excessive rounded corners
- Cheap-looking shadows
- Unnecessary animations
- “Tech startup” aesthetics
- Anything that makes the store feel like a template

The design should feel expensive because of its **simplicity, spacing, typography, composition, imagery, interaction design, and attention to detail**, not because of excessive decorative elements.

VISUAL DIRECTION

Use a refined, contemporary color system inspired by understated luxury and modest fashion.

Consider:

- Warm/off-white or ivory backgrounds
- Deep neutral tones
- Black/charcoal typography
- Subtle muted accent colors
- A restrained royal accent color where appropriate
- Very subtle borders and separators
- Soft, controlled shadows

The exact palette can be explored by Relume, but it must remain sophisticated and restrained.

Typography should feel premium and editorial while remaining highly readable.

Use typography hierarchy deliberately:

- Strong editorial headings
- Clear product names
- Highly readable body text
- Distinct prices
- Subtle metadata
- Carefully designed buttons and labels

The brand name “The Haya Collection” should have a distinctive typographic treatment that feels like a real fashion brand rather than a generic store logo.

MOBILE-FIRST / PWA EXPERIENCE

Design mobile screens first.

The mobile experience should feel like a native shopping application.

Think in terms of:

- Thumb-friendly interactions
- Bottom navigation where appropriate
- Sticky contextual actions
- Bottom sheets
- Swipeable product galleries
- Horizontal product carousels
- Mobile-friendly filters
- Mobile-friendly sorting
- Persistent cart access
- Clear back navigation
- Gesture-friendly interactions
- Large touch targets
- Minimal unnecessary navigation depth
- Fast access to important actions

Do not simply shrink the desktop design onto mobile.

The mobile version should be intentionally designed around how people actually use shopping apps.

The PWA should feel polished enough that a user could forget they are technically using a website.

DESKTOP EXPERIENCE

Desktop should preserve the same visual language while taking advantage of the larger screen.

Use:

- Large editorial compositions
- Immersive product imagery
- Carefully balanced grids
- Sophisticated whitespace
- Multi-column layouts where useful
- Rich product discovery
- Elegant navigation
- Subtle hover interactions

Desktop should feel premium and spacious rather than simply having large amounts of empty space.

CORE PAGES

Design the following pages and experiences.

1. HOME PAGE

The homepage should immediately communicate what The Haya Collection represents.

Include:

- Elegant hero section
- Brand introduction
- Featured collection
- Featured products
- New arrivals
- Best sellers / popular products
- Curated collections
- Editorial fashion imagery
- Short brand story
- Quality/material/craftsmanship messaging
- Customer trust indicators
- Social proof
- Instagram/social media section
- Newsletter or customer update section
- Footer

The hero should not necessarily look like a conventional e-commerce banner.

Explore a more editorial approach where imagery, typography, product placement, and interaction work together.

The homepage should feel like entering a premium fashion brand rather than entering a product database.

2. SHOP / COLLECTION PAGE

Create a clean product discovery experience.

Include:

- Product grid
- Product images
- Product names
- Prices
- Sale/availability indicators where relevant
- Quick actions
- Wishlist/favorite interaction
- Filters
- Sorting
- Categories/collections
- Search
- Pagination or infinite loading depending on UX suitability

Products should have subtle hover/touch interactions.

For example:

- Image transition
- Secondary product image on hover
- Quick add
- Wishlist interaction
- Subtle product information reveal

Do not overload product cards.

3. CATEGORY / COLLECTION PAGE

Allow the brand to create curated collections such as:

- New Arrivals
- Featured
- Seasonal Collections
- Abayas
- Accessories
- Modest Wear
- Other future categories

Each collection should have its own visual identity while remaining part of the overall design system.

Support both editorial collection headers and product grids.

4. PRODUCT DETAIL PAGE

This is one of the most important pages.

It must be optimized for users arriving directly from Instagram.

Include:

- Large immersive product gallery
- Multiple product images
- Swipeable mobile gallery
- Product name
- Price
- Discount information when applicable
- Availability
- Variants
- Size selection where applicable
- Color selection where applicable
- Quantity selector
- Add to Cart
- Buy Now
- Wishlist
- Product description
- Materials/fabric information
- Product specifications
- Size guide where relevant
- Care instructions
- Shipping information
- Return/exchange information
- Estimated delivery information
- Trust indicators
- Customer reviews
- Related products
- Frequently bought together / complementary products
- Recently viewed products

The primary purchase action should remain easily accessible.

On mobile, consider a **sticky bottom purchase bar** containing important actions such as price and Add to Cart / Buy Now.

The product gallery should feel immersive and premium.

Product information should not overwhelm the customer. Use progressive disclosure, accordions, expandable sections, and contextual information where appropriate.

5. SEARCH EXPERIENCE

Design a modern search experience.

Include:

- Search overlay/page
- Instant search suggestions
- Product suggestions
- Recent searches
- Popular searches
- Category suggestions
- No-result experience
- Helpful alternative recommendations

Search should feel fast and intelligent.

6. CART

Create a clean cart experience.

Include:

- Product summary
- Product image
- Variant information
- Quantity controls
- Remove/save-for-later
- Price breakdown
- Discounts
- Shipping information
- Estimated total
- Checkout CTA
- Recommended complementary products

Avoid making the cart unnecessarily complicated.

On mobile, the cart should feel like a natural continuation of the shopping application.

7. CHECKOUT

Create a frictionless, trustworthy checkout experience.

Include:

- Contact information
- Delivery address
- Shipping method
- Order summary
- Payment method
- Discount/coupon support
- Final price breakdown
- Terms/consent where necessary
- Place Order CTA

Use a clean distraction-free checkout layout.

The checkout should communicate security and reliability without filling the screen with unnecessary trust badges.

8. ORDER CONFIRMATION

After purchase, create a polished confirmation experience.

Include:

- Success state
- Order number
- Order summary
- Amount paid
- Delivery information
- Estimated delivery date
- Track order CTA
- Continue shopping CTA
- Customer support information

Make this feel like a proper completion of the shopping journey rather than simply displaying “Order successful.”

9. ORDER TRACKING

Create a beautiful order tracking experience.

Include:

- Order number
- Product/order summary
- Current status
- Visual delivery timeline
- Order placed
- Payment confirmed
- Order processing
- Shipped
- In transit
- Out for delivery
- Delivered

Include courier/tracking information where applicable.

The tracking interface should be visually understandable at a glance, particularly on mobile.

10. ACCOUNT / PROFILE

Create a customer account area.

Include:

- Profile
- Orders
- Order details
- Track orders
- Saved addresses
- Wishlist
- Saved products
- Account preferences
- Notification preferences
- Logout

The account area should feel like part of the same mobile application.

11. WISHLIST

Create a clean wishlist experience.

Include:

- Saved products
- Product availability
- Price
- Quick add to cart
- Remove
- Product recommendations
- Empty wishlist state

12. ORDER HISTORY / ORDER DETAILS

Allow customers to inspect previous purchases.

Show:

- Order number
- Date
- Products
- Quantity
- Price
- Payment status
- Shipping status
- Tracking information
- Delivery address
- Reorder/buy again where appropriate

13. ABOUT THE BRAND

Create an editorial-style About page.

The page should tell the story of The Haya Collection.

Include:

- Brand philosophy
- Why the brand exists
- Meaning behind the name
- Design philosophy
- Craftsmanship/quality
- Imagery
- Founder/brand story if appropriate
- Commitment to customers

Do not make this look like a corporate “About Us” page.

It should feel like a fashion brand editorial.

14. CONTACT / CUSTOMER SUPPORT

Include:

- Shipping questions
- Returns/exchanges

18. LEGAL / POLICY PAGES

Design clean versions of:

IMPORTANT UX STATES

Do not design only the “happy path.”

Create UX patterns for:

- Empty cart
- Empty wishlist
- No search results
- Product unavailable
- Product out of stock
- Product coming soon
- Loading states
- Skeleton loaders
- Failed payment
- Payment processing
- Order processing
- Failed order
- Network error
- Invalid coupon
- Invalid address
- Session expiration
- Form validation
- Successful actions
- Add-to-cart confirmation
- Wishlist confirmation

These states should feel intentional and polished rather than like afterthoughts.

MICRO-INTERACTIONS

The website should use subtle micro-interactions throughout.

Examples:

- Buttons respond naturally when pressed
- Add-to-cart animation
- Product image transitions
- Wishlist heart animation
- Cart count animation
- Smooth filter transitions
- Accordion expansion
- Bottom-sheet transitions
- Page transitions
- Search suggestions appearing smoothly
- Toast notifications
- Skeleton loading transitions
- Image lazy-loading transitions
- Product card hover effects
- Navigation transitions
- Scroll-triggered editorial animations
- Sticky elements appearing/disappearing intelligently
- Checkout progress transitions

Animations should communicate state and hierarchy.

Do NOT use animation simply because animation is possible.

Everything should feel fast, purposeful and restrained.

The overall principle should be:

**“You notice the quality of the interaction, but you don't notice the animation itself.”**

TRANSITIONS

Use a consistent motion language across the application.

Transitions should be:

- Smooth
- Short
- Predictable
- Contextual
- Physically believable
- Subtle

Avoid:

- Excessive bouncing
- Long cinematic transitions
- Distracting parallax
- Constant movement
- Animation overload

PERFORMANCE

Although the design should feel futuristic, it must also feel fast.

Prioritize:

- Lightweight visual effects
- Efficient image presentation
- Lazy loading
- Progressive content loading
- Skeleton states
- Minimal layout shifts
- Fast navigation
- Clear loading feedback

The interface should never feel slow because it is trying to look fancy.

Define patterns for:

- Buttons

- Product cards

- Badges
- Tags
- Alerts

- Modals
- Bottom sheets
- Accordions
- Navigation
- Tabs
- Breadcrumbs
- Cards

The same components should behave consistently throughout the application.

RESPONSIVE DESIGN

Design at least:

- Mobile
- Tablet
- Desktop

Mobile is the primary design target.

The responsive experience should not simply scale components.

Components should adapt intelligently depending on available space.

For example:

- Desktop navigation can become bottom navigation or a compact mobile navigation system.
- Desktop filter sidebars can become mobile filter sheets.
- Desktop product galleries can become swipeable galleries.
- Desktop multi-column checkout can become a focused single-column flow.
- Desktop hover interactions should have appropriate touch equivalents.

NAVIGATION

Design navigation around user intent rather than around the database structure.

The user should always understand:

- Where they are
- What they can do
- How to go back
- What they can purchase
- What is currently in their cart

The navigation should remain minimal and elegant.

Consider a mobile bottom navigation containing only the most important destinations, such as:

- Home
- Shop
- Search
- Wishlist
- Account

Cart should remain highly visible without unnecessarily consuming navigation space.

FUTURE-READY ARCHITECTURE FROM A DESIGN PERSPECTIVE

Although this is initially a small personal e-commerce brand, do not design it as a tiny temporary store.

The system should be capable of eventually supporting:

- Multiple categories
- Larger product catalogs
- Multiple collections
- Seasonal campaigns
- Promotional campaigns
- Discount codes
- Product variants
- Reviews
- Customer accounts
- Order tracking
- Personalized recommendations
- Editorial content
- Social commerce
- Future expansion into additional fashion categories

The design should therefore be scalable without becoming unnecessarily complex today.

OVERALL EXPERIENCE

The final result should feel like:

A premium fashion brand + a modern shopping application + an editorial fashion experience.

It should retain conventional e-commerce patterns so customers never feel lost, but improve almost every interaction through thoughtful UX.

The guiding principle is:

**Familiar enough to understand immediately. Innovative enough to feel like the next generation of e-commerce.**

The user should be able to discover a product from Instagram, understand the product within seconds, explore it beautifully, make a purchase with minimal friction, receive clear confirmation, and later track the order — all within one cohesive experience.

The final design should make someone think:

**“This feels like a real premium fashion brand with a genuinely modern shopping experience.”**

Prioritize clarity, elegance, speed, trust, usability, accessibility, responsive behavior, and exceptional attention to detail over decorative complexity.
